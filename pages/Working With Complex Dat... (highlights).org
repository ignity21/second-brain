** Working with complex data is hard.

But there is a way to ease the pain.

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) can simplify complex data.

I will explain the basics now.

1/7 

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/Fp4qvynaEAAcEZO.jpg) ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/levikul09/status/1629787121358868480))
** In real life, many datasets have high dimensionality.

What does that mean?

They have a lot of features.

But the more feature the harder our work is.

Simplicity is good.

But how can we simplify complex datasets?

2/7 

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/Fp4qwS3aYAABPuR.jpg) ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/levikul09/status/1629787130187661313))
** The technique is called dimensionality reduction.

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is one of these techniques.

PCA can reduce the number of features in a dataset.

During the process, PCA tries to retain as much information as possible.

3/7 

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/Fp4qw0vaMAIxDa6.jpg) ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/levikul09/status/1629787140023283715))
** Let's consider this example:

You are on the beach at the ocean.

It is a hot summer day, you are drinking a cocktail and enjoying the vibe when the sun goes down.

You want to take this vibe with you, so you take a picture.

4/7 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/levikul09/status/1629787144549199872))
** Of course, the picture will never give back the full experience.

But when you look at it, you may get a little back from the vibe.

What did you do with the picture?

Reduced dimensionality.

From the 3D world, you created a 2D image.

5/7 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/levikul09/status/1629787147333951488))
** Why do we use PCA?

- With fewer features, we can decrease the training time of a model.

- We remove the unnecessary features

- We can reduce overfitting.
