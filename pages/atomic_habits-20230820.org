#+title: Atomic Habits
#+filetags: Book

* Definition
- Habit :: a routine or behavior that is performed regularly and *automatically*.
- Atomic Habit :: refers to a tiny change, a marginal gain, a 1% improvement.

* Four Laws
** Creating Positive Habits
- Make it obvious
- Make it attractive
- Make it easy
- Make it satisfying

** Breaking Bad Habits
- Make it invisible
- Make it unattractive
- Make it difficult
- Make it unsatisfying

* Identity
When most people try to adopt new habits, they focus on what they want to achieve (the outcome)
and how they can achieve it (the process).
- e.g. outcome: losing 15 pounds; process: going to gym

Improvements like these are temporary unless they form a part of your *identity*.
- A person believes in a particular aspect of their identity, they are more likely to act in alignment with that belief.
- To truly stick to our habits, we need to focus on changing our identity, not just the specific outcomes and processes.

- *The goal is not to read a book, the goal is to become a reader.*
- *The goal is not to learn an instrument, the goal is to become a musician.*

** Example
- Finding Identity
  - "Who is the type of person that could lose 40 pounds?"
  - The goal wouldn't be to lose weight, but to become a healthier person.
- Inform Your Decisions
  - "What would a healthy person do?" -> Prove it.

* Habit Loop
1. cue: tells your brain to do something
2. craving: what motivates your action
3. response: the habit you perform
4. reward: the end result of your habit

** Example
1. cue: your phone buzzing with a new text
2. craving: the desire to see what someone said to you
3. response: picking up the phone check
4. reward: comes when you satisfy your craving to read the message

* Practical Tips
- Make it obvious
  - Make new habits more obvious by attaching them to habits you already have. (*Habit Stacking*)
  - Frame it: After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]
- Make it attractive
  - Make hard habits more attractive by linking an action you *like to do* with an action you *need to do*. (*Temptation Bundling*)
  - After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [HABIT I NEED], Then, I will [HABIT I WANT].
- Make it easy
  - Make your habits easier by creating an environment that encourages good habits and discourages bad habits.
  - e.g. If you want exercise more, pack your gym bag ahead of time and set out your exercise clothes.
  - e.g. If you want to improve your diet chop up fruits and vegetables so when you get hungry they're easily accessible.
  - e.g. bad habit: Shuts down router at 10:00 p.m. (Make it difficult)
- Make it satisfying
  - e.g. Paper Clip Strategy, a visual way to track your progress
  - Just use streaks
