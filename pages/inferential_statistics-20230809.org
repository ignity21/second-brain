* Basics
** Symbols
| $\mu$          | population mean(expected value) |
| $\sigma$          | population standard deviation   |
| $\hat\mu$    | estimated population mean       |
| $\hat\sigma$ | estimated population sd         |
| $\bar{X_i}$  | one sample $X_i$'s mean         |
| $s$          | sample sd                       |
| $n$          | sample size                     |
- Bessel's correction $\hat\sigma=\sqrt\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\bar{x})}{n-1}$

** Degrees of Freedom
- Example1: You have to choose n numbers from all natural numbers.($DF=n$)
- Example2: You have n numbers that must sum to 10. ($DF=n-1$)
- Example3: $DF=4$
  [[../assets/Statistics/DegreesOfFreedom.png]]

- Related to estimated population sd
  - $x_1+x_2+...+x_n=\bar{x}\cdot{n}$ (to get $\bar{x}\cdot{n}$ -> $DF=n-1$)
  - Only $n-1$ values are independent *after* we know the mean.

* Relative Errors (on Sample Size)
- Relative errors: \(error\%=100\times\frac{|\mu-\bar{X_{i}}|}{\mu}\)

* Sampling Distribution
- Sampling Distribution :: Distribution of sample means
| $X_i$                | Variable in Sample i                                        |
| $\bar{X_i}$          | sample mean                                                 |
| $n$                  | number of samples                                           |
| $M$                  | mean of sample means                                        |
| $SE$(Standard Error) | standard deviation of the statistic of interest (mean here) |

- $M=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{X_i}$
- Ratio of $\sigma$ to $SE$: $\frac{\sigma}{SE}=\sqrt{n}$
** [[id:fd7b8935-0094-403b-ac49-a61c8df0a733][Confidence Intervals]]

#+begin_src python

* Z-Distribution

  - the mean is 0

** Confidence Interval Bounds
| z-score(critical value) |  CI |
|-------------------------+-----|
| $\pm 1.96$                | 95% |
| $\pm 2.33$                | 98% |
- Margin of Error: $z\times SE$. it's half the width of the confidence interval.
  - e.g. for 95% CI: Margin of Error $= 1.96\times SE = 1.96\times\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$
- $\bar{X_i}$ should be in $(\mu-1.96SE, \mu+1.96SE)$, then 95% Confidence Interval is:
  - $\bar{X_i}-1.96SE<\mu<\bar{X_i}+1.96SE$
  - 95% CI with the bigger sample size -> the smaller CI bounds

** $\alpha$ Levels
- $\alpha$ Levels of Likelihood.
- $\alpha$ level is a criterion for deciding whether or not something is likely or unlikely.
  - 5% (generally used)
  - 1%
  - 0.1%
