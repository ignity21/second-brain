#+title: Macroeconomic Drivers of Markets
#+filetags: :Trading:

* Economic Cycle
Expansion → peak → contraction → trough → expansion. A strong economy shows:
- rising output (GDP);
- falling unemployment;
- rising inflation (moderate, from demand);
- rising business investment (capital expenditure);
- political stability, which lets businesses plan and invest.

Markets price the *expected* cycle, so stocks usually turn before the economy does. That is why the S&P 500 is itself a leading indicator (see [[*Composite Indexes][Composite Indexes]]).

* Monetary Policy
The central bank controls the money supply and short-term interest rates.
- Lower rates → cheaper credit → more borrowing and spending → a stronger economy, and higher stock valuations because future earnings are discounted less.
- Higher rates → the reverse; used to fight inflation.
- US: the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meets 8 times a year and sets the target range for the federal funds rate (see [[id:167c14ef-51ed-4a7c-9cb6-b6906fee366d][Money Market Instruments]]). It also sets rules for banks. Rate decisions and the guidance about future decisions move all markets.

* Fiscal Policy
The government taxes and spends.
- Lower taxes or more spending → more money in circulation → stimulates the economy, at the cost of a larger deficit.
- Higher taxes → less money in circulation → slows the economy, but can pay down government debt.

* Scheduled Reports
Markets react to the *surprise*: the actual number minus the consensus forecast. A good number that was expected moves nothing. Release times and consensus values are on an economic calendar such as Bloomberg's.

| Report | Frequency (ET) | Measures | Typical reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report | Wednesday 10:30 | US crude and product inventory changes | Inventory draw → more demand or less supply → oil up |
| Initial Jobless Claims | Thursday 8:30 | New unemployment insurance claims | Rising claims → weakening labor market |
| NAHB Housing Market Index | Monthly | Home builders' view of the single-family housing market | Rising → housing demand, and spending on furniture and appliances follows |
| Consumer Sentiment (University of Michigan) | Monthly | Households' attitude toward near-term spending | Rising → consumers likely to spend |

* Composite Indexes
The Conference Board publishes both monthly (around the 20th):

| Index | Role | Components (current list) |
|---|---|---|
| Leading Economic Index (LEI) | Moves before the economy; anticipates turning points by months | Average weekly manufacturing hours, initial jobless claims, new orders (consumer goods, nondefense capital goods, ISM new orders), building permits, S&P 500, Leading Credit Index, 10-year minus fed funds spread, consumer expectations |
| Coincident Economic Index (CEI) | Moves with the economy; confirms where it is now | Nonfarm payroll employment, personal income less transfers, industrial production, manufacturing and trade sales |

The LEI component list changes over time: money supply and supplier deliveries were dropped in earlier revisions.
