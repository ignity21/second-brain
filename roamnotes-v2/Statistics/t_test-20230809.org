#+title: T-Test
#+filetags: :Statistics:Math:

* Overview
- t statistic follows a [[id:1bf89458-2878-4fbf-a0b9-9a59a8fdc486][T-Distribution]]

* Types
- One sample t-test :: test the mean of one group against a constant value.
- Two sample t-test :: test the difference of means between two groups.
- Paired sample t-test :: test the difference of means between two measurements of the same subject.

* One-sample t-test
Since we don't know the population standard deviation $\sigma$, we use sample standard deviation $\hat\sigma$ to calculate $SE$. (use bootstrapping)
- It's more prone to error.
- It's more spread out and thicker in the tails than a normal distribution.
[[file:../assets/Statistics/t-Distribution.png]]
$$t=\frac{\hat\mu-\mu_0}{\hat\sigma/\sqrt{n}}$$
- $\mu_0$ is some specified value.
- $H_0$: $\mu = \mu_0$
- $H_A$: $\mu < \mu_0$, $\mu > \mu_0$, $\mu \neq \mu_0$
  - Cohen's d: $d=\frac{\hat\mu-\mu_0}{\hat\sigma}$

* Two Sample t-test
- \(H_{0}\): \(\mu_{1} - \mu_{2} = 0\)
- \(H_{A}\):
  - two-tailed: \(\mu_{1} - \mu_{2} \ne 0\)
  - left-tailed: \(\mu_{1} - \mu_{2} < 0\)
  - right-tailed: \(\mu_{1} - \mu_{2} > 0\)
- \(t=\frac{\text{diff in sample stats}-\text{diff in pop stats}}{\text{standard error}}\)
  - \(t=\frac{(\bar{x}_{1} - \bar{x}_{2}) - (\mu_{1} - \mu_{2})}{SE(\bar{x}_{1} - \bar{x}_{2})}\)
  - Assume \(H_{0}\) is True: \(t=\frac{(\bar{x}_{1} - \bar{x}_{2})}{SE(\bar{x}_{1} - \bar{x}_{2})}\)
** Calculating SE
  - Bootstrapping is a good option
  - Easier way to use an *approximation*: \(SE(\bar{x}_{1} - \bar{x}_{2})\approx\sqrt{\frac{s^{2}_{1}}{n_{1}}+\frac{s^{2}_{2}}{n_{2}}}\)
    - \(s\) is the std of the variable
    - \(n\) is the sample size (num of observations/rows in sample)

** T Statistic
\[t=\frac{(\bar{x}_{1} - \bar{x}_{2})}{\sqrt{\frac{s^{2}_{1}}{n_{1}}+\frac{s^{2}_{2}}{n_{2}}}}\]

** Degree of Freedom
\[DF=n_{1} + n_{2} - 2\]
- because we know two sample statistics, the means for each group.

** Calculating P-values
- left-tailed: ~p_value = t.cdf(t_stat, df)~
- right-tailed: ~p_value = 1 - t.cdf(t_stat, df)~
- two-tailed: use critical value

* Paired Sample t-test
- Dependent t-test
  - Compares the means of two measurements taken from the same individual, object, or related units
  - e.g. pre-, post-tests are given to the same random sample of students
- \(\mu_{diff}=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\)
- \(H_{0}\): \(\mu_{diff}=0\)
- \(H_{A}\): \(\mu_{diff}<0\) left-tailed
- \(t=\frac{\bar{x}_{diff}-\mu_{diff}}{\sqrt{\frac{s^{2}_{diff}}{n_{diff}}}}\)
  - \(df=n_{diff} - 1\): only have one statistic

* Examples
** Two Sample Right-tailed
- ~converted_comp~ is a numerical variable
- ~age_first_code_cut~ is a categorical variable with levels ("child" and "adult")
- Compare sample across groups ~age_first_code_cut~
- Question: Are users in "child" group compensated higher than "adult"
- \(H_{0}\): The mean compensation is the same \(\mu_{child}=\mu_{adult}\)
  - \(\mu_{child}-\mu_{adult}=0\)
- \(H_{A}\): The mean compensation in "child" group is greater than in "adult" \(\mu_{child}>\mu_{adult}\)
  - \(\mu_{child}-\mu_{adult}>0\)
- Test statistic is \(\mu_{child} - \mu_{adult}\)
#+begin_src python
# Two sample means 'child' and 'adult'
xbar = stack_overflow.groupby('age_first_code_cut')['converted_comp'].mean()
s = stack_overflow.groupby('age_first_code_cut')['converted_comp'].std()
n = stack_overflow.groupby('age_first_code_cut')['converted_comp'].count()

t_numerator = xbar.loc['child'] - xbar.loc['adult']
t_denominator = np.sqrt(s.loc['child']**2/n.loc['child'] + s.loc['adult']**2/n.loc['adult'])
t_stat = t_numerator / t_denominator

degrees_of_freedom = n.loc['child'] + n.loc['adult'] - 2

p_value = 1 - stats.t.cdf(t_stat, df=degrees_of_freedom)
# if p_value < 0.05, we reject the null hypothesis
#+end_src

** Paired Sample Left-tailed
#+begin_src python
# paired t-test
sample_data['diff'] = sample_data['2024'] - sample_data['2021']
xbar_diff = sample_data['diff'].mean()
n_diff = len(sample_data['diff'])
s_diff = sample_data['diff'].std(df=1)
t_stat = (xbar_diff - 0) / np.sqrt(s_diff**2/n_diff)
degrees_of_freedom = n_diff - 1

# left-tailed
p_value = t.cdf(t_stat, df=degrees_of_freedom)
#+end_src
