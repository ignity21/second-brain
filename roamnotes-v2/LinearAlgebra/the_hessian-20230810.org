#+title: The Hessian
#+filetags: :LinearAlgebra:Math:

[[id:661e1889-ce72-4cbe-a0ca-7a88944a72e5][The Jacobian]] is the first order derivative. (vector)

The Hessian is the second order derivative. (matrix)

* An Example
$$f(x,y,z)=x^2 yz$$

$$J=\begin{bmatrix}2xyz & x^{2}z & x^{2}y\end{bmatrix}$$

$$H=\begin{bmatrix}2yz & 2xz & 2xy \\
2xz & 0 & x^2 \\
2xy & x^2 & 0\end{bmatrix}$$

* Second Derivative Test
At a critical point (the Jacobian is zero), the eigenvalues of $H$ decide:
- All positive: local minimum.
- All negative: local maximum.
- Mixed signs: saddle point.
- Some zero: the test is inconclusive.

For two variables this reduces to: $det(H) > 0$ and $H_{11} > 0$ → minimum; $det(H) > 0$ and $H_{11} < 0$ → maximum; $det(H) < 0$ → saddle point.

The two-variable shortcut does not extend to three variables. The example above at $(1, 1, 1)$ gives $det(H) = 6 > 0$ and $H_{11} = 2 > 0$, but the eigenvalues are about $-1.37$, $-1$ and $4.37$, so it would be a saddle point (checked with NumPy 2.5.4, 2026-10-27).
