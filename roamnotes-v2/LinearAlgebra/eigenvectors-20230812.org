#+title: Eigenvectors
#+filetags: LinearAlgebra

* Definition
An *eigenvector* of a *square matrix* $M$ is a non-zero vector that remains on the same line after transformation by the linear transformation
\[M\cdot\bm{v}=\lambda \times \bm{v}\]
Where $\lambda$ is a scalar value called the eigenvalue associated to the vector $\bm{v}$.

* Finding Eigenvalues
\[M\cdot\bm{v}=\lambda \times (I\cdot \bm{v})\]

\[M\cdot\bm{v}-(\lambda\times I)\cdot \bm{v}=0\]

\[(M-\lambda\times I)\cdot \bm{v}=0\]

- $det(M-\lambda\times I)$ should be 0 to transform a non-zero $\bm{v}$ into $\vec{0}$
** Application
- To calculate $M^{10}$

\[M=\begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 \\ 0 & 2\end{bmatrix}\]

\[M-\lambda\times I=\begin{bmatrix} 3-\lambda & 1 \\ 0 & 2-\lambda\end{bmatrix}\]
*** Finding Eigenvalues
\[det(M-\lambda\times I)=(3-\lambda)(2-\lambda)-1\times 0=0\]

- The eigenvalues are (3, 2)

*** Finding Eigenvectors
- $\lambda_1=3$: $(M-3I)\bm{v}=\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}\bm{v}=\bm{0}$ gives $\bm{v}_1=\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$
- $\lambda_2=2$: $(M-2I)\bm{v}=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}\bm{v}=\bm{0}$ gives $\bm{v}_2=\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$

*** Computing $M^{10}$
Put the eigenvectors in the columns of $P$ and the eigenvalues on the diagonal of $D$; then $M=PDP^{-1}$, and the inner $P^{-1}P$ factors cancel in every power:

\[M^{10}=PD^{10}P^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3^{10} & 0 \\ 0 & 2^{10}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}59049 & 58025 \\ 0 & 1024\end{bmatrix}\]

Here $P^{-1}=P$. Powering a diagonal matrix only powers its diagonal entries. Checked with NumPy 2.5.4 ~matrix_power~ (2026-10-27).
