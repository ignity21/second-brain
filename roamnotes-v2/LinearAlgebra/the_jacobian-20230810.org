#+title: The Jacobian
#+filetags: :LinearAlgebra:Math:

* Jacobian Vector
- Partial derivatives: f respect to t
$$\frac{df}{dt}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dt}$$

- Given function: $f(x_1, x_2, x_3, ...)$

- The Jacobian vector is

$$J=[\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1},\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_2},\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_3},...]$$

* Jacobian Matrix

$$u(x,y)=x-2y$$

$$v(x,y)=3y-2x$$

$$J_u=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\end{bmatrix}$$

$$J_v=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\end{bmatrix}$$

$$J=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} \\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 \\ -2 & 3\end{bmatrix}$$

The gradients are constant everywhere.

* Multivariate Chain Rule
When $f$ depends on $t$ through a vector $\vec{x}(t)$ , the sum above becomes a product of the Jacobian row vector and the derivative vector:

$$\frac{df}{dt}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial \vec{x}}\frac{d\vec{x}}{dt}$$

With one more layer, $\vec{x}(\vec{u}(t))$ , insert the Jacobian matrix of $\vec{x}$ with respect to $\vec{u}$ :

$$\frac{df}{dt}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial \vec{x}}\frac{\partial \vec{x}}{\partial \vec{u}}\frac{d\vec{u}}{dt}$$

Each factor is a Jacobian, and the shapes chain like matrix multiplication: $(1 \times n)(n \times m)(m \times 1)$ . Backpropagation in neural networks is this product evaluated from the left, one layer at a time.
