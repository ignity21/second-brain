#+title: R-squared
#+filetags: :Statistics:ML:

* Overview
- known as "Coefficient of Determination"
- be used to evaluate the performance of regression models

* Definition
- *R-squared* is the proportion of the variance in the response variable
  that is predictable from the explanatory variable
- \(R^2=1-\frac{SS_{res}}{SS_{tot}}\)
- \(SS_{res}=\sum(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2=\)
  - \(\hat{y}_i\): predicted value from the model.
  - Residual Sum of Squares
    - measures the variance in dependent variable that is not explained by the independent variable
- \(SS_{tot}=\sum(y_i-\bar{y})^2=\)
  - \(\bar{y}\): mean of the actual values
  - Total Sum of Squares
    - measures the total variance in the dependent variable

* Interpretation
- range from 0 to 1
  - =1= means a perfect fit
  - =0= means the worst possible fit
- Higher R-squared values indicate a better fit for the model
- Low R-squared values indicate a poor model fit.

* Limitations
- Not Always Indicative of Model Quality: A high R-squared does not necessarily mean that the model
  is good. It can be misleading, especially in the presence of overfitting.
- Does Not Indicate Causation: R-squared only indicates correlation and not causation.
- Sensitive to Outliers: Outliers can disproportionately affect the R-squared value.

* Code
- statsmodels: ~model.rsquared~
- scikit-learn: ~model.score(X_)~
