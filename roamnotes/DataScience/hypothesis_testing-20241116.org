** Significance Level \(\alpha\)
The significance level of a hypothesis test is the threshold point of "beyond a reasonable doubt"
- Cutoff point between a small p-value and a large one
- Should be set prior to conducting the hypothesis test
- Common values of \(\alpha\) are 0.2, 0.1, *0.05* and 0.01
- Common to choose CI level of \(1-\alpha\)

** Critical Value
- z-score of the \(\alpha\) level

* Define Hypothesis
<<Define Hypothesis>>
- $H_0$ (Null Hypothesis): $\mu_{1}\approx\mu_{2}$ (no significant difference)
  - $\mu_{2}$ is not in critical region

- $H_a$ (Alternative Hypothesis), or $H_{1}$
  - $\mu_{2}$ is in critical region
  - $\mu_{1}\not\approx\mu_{2}$
  - $\mu_{1}\lt\mu_{2}$  $\mu_2$ is in the one-tailed right critical region
  - $\mu_{1}\gt\mu_{2}$ one-tailed left critical region
  - $\mu_{1}\neq\mu_{2}$ two-tailed

* Choose the Appropriate Test (2 Groups)
<<Choose Test>>
[[../assets/Statistics/InferentialStats_1.png]]

- Z-Test: compare two groups/categories of numeric variables with large sample size
- T-Test: compare two groups/categories of numeric variables with small sample size
- ANOVA test: compare the difference between two or more groups/categories of numeric variables
- Chi-Squared test: examine the relationship between two categorical variables
- Correlation test: examine the relationship between two numeric variables

** Example
- $H_0$: Most dogs have four legs. (Most = more than 50%)
- $H_A$: Most dogs have less than four legs.(one-tailed Test)
  - Sample: 10 dogs, all have four legs. (fail to reject $H_0$)
  - Sample: 10 dogs, 6 dogs have three legs. (reject $H_0$)

** Statistical Decision Errors
|                  | actual \(H_{0}\)             | actual \(H_{A}\)              |
| chosen \(H_{0}\) | Correct                      | False negative(Type II Error) |
| chosen \(H_{A}\) | False positive(Type I Error) | Correct                       |
