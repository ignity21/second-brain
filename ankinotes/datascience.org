*** Front
After training a polynomial regression model, J_train is high and J_cv ≈ J_train (both are high). Is this high bias or high variance?

*** Back
High bias (underfitting). High bias shows up as J_train itself being high, with J_cv close to J_train; high variance instead shows a low J_train but a J_cv much larger than J_train.

** Raising the classification threshold's effect on Precision/Recall
:PROPERTIES:
:ANKI_NOTE_TYPE: CC Basic
:ANKI_NOTE_ID: 1789812762988
:ANKI_NOTE_HASH: f4de9c97ed473ef2e990fa48e8372a15

*** Front
In a logistic regression rare-disease classifier, raising the prediction threshold from 0.5 to 0.7 — how do precision and recall change, and why?

*** Back
Precision goes up, recall goes down. A higher threshold means the model only predicts positive when very confident (predicted probability ≥ 0.7), so false positives drop (precision ↑), but more actual positive cases that fall below the confidence bar get missed, so false negatives rise (recall ↓).

** Shrinkage term in regularized gradient descent
:PROPERTIES:
:ANKI_NOTE_TYPE: CC Basic
:ANKI_NOTE_ID: 1789812762994
:ANKI_NOTE_HASH: e439ac824a039cd891ab218c6ccfaf31
:END:

*** Front
For j ≥ 1, the gradient descent update for regularized linear regression can be rewritten as $\theta_j \leftarrow (1 - \alpha\lambda)\theta_j - \frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \bigl(h_\theta(x^{(i)}) - y^{(i)}\bigr)x_j^{(i)}$. How does this form make regularization's “penalize large coefficients” effect intuitive?

*** Back
Before the usual gradient update, $\theta_j$ is multiplied by the shrinkage factor $(1 - \alpha\lambda)$, which is slightly less than 1. This pulls $\theta_j$ a little toward zero at every step; a larger $\lambda$ produces stronger shrinkage and therefore resists large coefficients.

Source: Gradient Descent

** Choosing between GridSearchCV and RandomizedSearchCV
:PROPERTIES:
:ANKI_NOTE_TYPE: CC Basic
:ANKI_NOTE_ID: 1789812762996
:ANKI_NOTE_HASH: e1a4cf99b211fb7e2b7ae1ca9f2b23c3
:END:

*** Front
When there are many hyperparameters and each has many candidate values, why is RandomizedSearchCV usually preferred over GridSearchCV?

*** Back
GridSearchCV exhaustively fits every combination, so the number of fits grows fast with the number of combinations; RandomizedSearchCV instead samples a fixed number of random combinations from the space, reaching a near-optimal result with far fewer fits, making it a better fit for high-dimensional searches with many candidate values.
